## Q1 · Incentives

The NTSB is deliberately built with no dog in the fight. It does not regulate the airlines, certify aircraft, or bring enforcement actions, so it has no revenue or turf that a given finding could threaten. Its sole output is a determination of what happened. Who pays it and for what is a public agency budget, and it explains why its findings are trusted even by the parties they criticize.

## Q2 · Corrections

This is the essay's model for corrections done right. Every crash and every consequential near miss gets an independent investigation, the findings are published, and the rules change in response. Blame is often explicitly set aside, because blame makes people hide things and hidden things crash the next plane. Run over decades, that loop is a large part of why flying went from routine disaster to the safest way to travel.

## Q3 · Verification

The NTSB publishes not just conclusions but the underlying dockets, so its reasoning is checkable by outsiders. For the aviation system as a whole, the NTSB investigation *is* the outside verification: the body checking the airlines and manufacturers does not work for them.

## Q4 · Exit

There is no exit, by design. You cannot hire a competing national investigator. As with any singular institution, the substitute for competition is transparency, which is exactly what it produces.
