## Q1 · Incentives

Meeting the diesel NOx standard at the volume and price VW wanted was hard; a defeat device that detected the test and cleaned up only for it was cheap. The incentive pointed straight at cheating the measurement rather than the emissions, and that is what the engineering delivered.

## Q2 · Corrections

The cheating was not caught by any internal process, and not by the regulator's own lab tests, which the cars passed. It was caught by engineers at West Virginia University, working on a roughly $69,000 contract from the nonprofit ICCT, driving rented VWs around California with a portable tester because the road numbers did not match the lab numbers. One car exceeded the standard by a factor of fifteen to thirty-five. Even then it took a year, with VW calling the discrepancy a technical glitch, before the EPA issued its Notice of Violation in September 2015.

## Q3 · Verification

This is the cleanest example of gameable verification. The official lab test was real, independent, and worthless here, because the device was built to recognize and pass exactly that test. Verification that the subject can detect and defeat is not verification. The honest check was the one nobody was required to run: independent measurement on the actual road.
