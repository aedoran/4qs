## Q1 · Incentives

The Fed is the essay's showcase for design that never depends on anyone being good. Governors serve 14-year staggered terms, one expiring every two years, and the system funds itself through its operations rather than through money Congress appropriates. Both features exist to make the obvious betrayal, inflating the economy to help whoever is in power, structurally hard and publicly visible. The founders' phrase for the move is "ambition must be made to counteract ambition." What makes it trustworthy is the arrangement, not the character of the current chair.

## Q2 · Corrections

The Fed publishes FOMC minutes about three weeks after each meeting and full transcripts after a five-year lag, which makes its reasoning inspectable in arrears. But its review of its own policy misses is largely self-assessed, with no independent body second-guessing monetary judgment. This is left `unrated` pending a closer look at how it handled specific errors.

## Q3 · Verification

The Fed is overseen by Congress and its financial statements are audited by an independent auditor, but its core product, monetary-policy decisions, is not verified by any outside authority. Left `unrated` pending a fuller assessment.

## Q4 · Exit

There is no exit, by design. You cannot choose a different central bank. That is not a failure; it is what "singular institution" means. When exit is off the table, the entire weight of trust falls on legible incentives, visible corrections, and outside oversight.

## Timeline
